# Tokens, summon vs play, and players as targets: verified facts (session 04c)

Researcher report, 2026-10-03. Verdicts per claim, with the quoted passage and source.

## MTG
Source: https://yawgatog.com/resources/magic-rules/ ("effective as of September 25, 2026")

- 111.1: "Some effects put tokens onto the battlefield. A token is a marker used to represent any permanent that isn't represented by a card." 111.6: "A token isn't a card (even if represented by a card that has a Magic back…)."
- 111.7: "A token that's in a zone other than the battlefield ceases to exist. This is a state-based action; see rule 704. (Note that if a token changes zones, applicable triggered abilities will trigger before the token ceases to exist.)"
- 111.8: "A token that has left the battlefield can't move to another zone or come back onto the battlefield. If such a token would change zones, it remains in its current zone instead. It ceases to exist the next time state-based actions are checked."
- 704.5d: "If a token is in a zone other than the battlefield, it ceases to exist."
- A dying token reaches the graveyard briefly, so its "dies" triggers fire.
- 115.4: "Some spells and abilities that refer to damage require "any target," "another target," "two targets," or similar rather than "target [something]." These targets may be creatures, players, planeswalkers, or battles. Other game objects, such as noncreature artifacts or spells, can't be chosen."
- 119.2: "Damage dealt to a player normally causes that player to lose that much life." 120.3a: "Damage dealt to a player by a source without infect causes that player to lose that much life." 120.3b: infect gives poison counters instead.
- 109.1: "An object is an ability on the stack, a card, a copy of a card, a token, a spell, a permanent, or an emblem." 102.1: "A player is one of the people in the game." Players aren't objects because 109.1 omits them. Starting life is 20 (103.4).

## Hearthstone

- Battlecry (https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Battlecry): "Battlecry is an ability where a particular effect activates when the card with the Battlecry is played directly from the hand." "Battlecries do not activate if the card is brought into play by another card. For example, Alarm-o-Bot, Faceless Manipulator and Mirror Entity all place minions onto the board, but any Battlecries will not be activated." Exceptions repeat Battlecries without a play (Shudderwock).
- Summon (https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Summon): "All minions and locations are considered to be "summoned". However, only minions and locations played directly from the hand are considered to have been "played". Card text which states "plays a minion" therefore refers only to the direct playing of a minion from the hand, while "summons a minion" can refer to a minion being summoned by any means. For example, using Reinforce to summon a Silver Hand Recruit will not trigger Mirror Entity, but will trigger Sword of Justice." "Minions summoned by a summon effect are not played directly from the hand, and therefore will not trigger Battlecries or Overload. However, they will work with triggered effects which respond to the summoning of minions." "Transform effects … do not trigger summoning-related effects."
- Return to hand (https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Return_to_hand): "The target returns to its original card form, complete with mana cost, and becomes part of the player's hand." "Return effects can be used on uncollectible cards that cannot otherwise be found in a player's hand, such as the Sheep generated by Polymorph… These cards have mana costs and function just like any other card in hand." With a full hand (10), the returned minion is destroyed and counts as dying.
- Deaths (https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Advanced_rulebook): "Minions that are killed and removed from play … are moved to the "Graveyard Zone"." "Rule DEC1: Hearthstone keeps track of every Death Event that has been created this game in the Death Event Cache." "Rule DEC3: Cards that check if a minion has died this turn/… (Resurrect, Kel'Thuzad, N'Zoth…) do not check the Graveyard Zone. They check the Death Event Cache." Tokens are not named, but nothing excludes them (inference). Resurrect: "summon a random minion from all friendly minions that have died this game".
- Hero (https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Hero): "In Constructed, heroes have 30 base Health… Heroes by default do not have an Attack value, but can use various means to gain Attack such as equipping weapons. Heroes with an Attack value can attack other targets, similarly to minions…" "Heroes cannot be targeted by spells or effects that specify "minion" rather than "character"." Armor (https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Armor): "Damage taken will always be deducted from Armor before Health."
- Hero replacement (Advanced rulebook): "Whenever you replace your hero with a new one, in general, everything that is an attribute of or an enchantment on your previous hero is gone, and the following things happen: Damage, Health, and Armor are kept… You are unfrozen. All temporary enchantments are destroyed… You keep your weapon, unless the replacement specifies equipping a new one, such as Lord Jaraxxus… Your hero's attack is not refreshed. Your Hero Power is replaced…" "First your hero is replaced by a Hero with the same current Health and maximum Health and gain Armor displayed on the card." Lord Jaraxxus (https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Lord_Jaraxxus): his Battlecry "always sets the hero's Health to 15, whereas hero cards retain the hero's existing Health".
- Hero entity ID after replacement: unverified. HearthSim's protocol doc says every deck card is an entity from game start, so a played hero card is most likely its own existing entity (inference). The Fireplace simulator models Classic Jaraxxus as summoning a new hero entity (`Summon(CONTROLLER, "EX1_323h")`), per https://hearthsim.info/blog/happy-feast-of-winter-veil-champion/.

## Yu-Gi-Oh

- Official Rulebook v10 (https://img.yugioh-card.com/en/downloads/rulebook/SD_RuleBook_EN_10.pdf): "Each player starts a Duel with 8000 LP (Life Points). You win a Duel if: you reduce your opponent's LP to 0…" "The amount that your attacking monster's ATK exceeds the ATK of your opponent's monster is subtracted from your opponent's LP as battle damage." No source says players aren't cards; none contradicts it.
- Tokens (rulebook p. 27): "Monster Tokens are monsters that appear on the field as the result of a card's effect. They are not included in the Deck, and cannot be sent anywhere other than the field, such as the hand or Graveyard. When a Token is destroyed or returned to the hand or Deck, they are simply removed from the field instead… Their battle position is always either face-up Attack Position or face-up Defense Position. Tokens cannot be used as an Xyz Material. Tokens on the field are treated as Normal Monsters."
- Yugipedia Monster Token (search excerpts, direct fetch 403): "If a Token leaves the field (i.e. it would be moved to the hand, Main Deck, or Graveyard, or is banished), it ceases to exist and is removed from the game. They are not considered to have been moved to any other location." "Since Tokens cannot be Normal Summoned or Set, they are considered Special Summon Monsters."
- Unverified: that a Token "is not a card". Only non-authoritative sites say so.
- Contrast: a YGO Token never reaches another location. An MTG token arrives there, then a state-based action removes it.
